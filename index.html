How Messina works

Quick start

  1. Put on headphones. Speakers plus an open mic will feed back.
  2. Press Start and allow microphone access (or switch Input to Audio file).
  3. Sing a note, then hold keys on the on-screen keyboard's letters — or hold space to play the chord chart.
  4. Bring Master (top bar) up slowly. esc mutes instantly — the header shows MUTED until you press it again.

Ways to play

Laptop keys. a to k are a piano octave, with the black keys on the row above. Hold several to stack harmonies. z / x shift the keys an octave.

Chord chart. Type or paste chords into the box — bar lines and line breaks are ignored, so you can paste straight from a tab site. Hold space to sound the highlighted chord; let go and the next one is cued. While space is held, ← → move straight to the neighbouring chord without a gap. With nothing held, the arrows just move the cue. delete rewinds, and clicking a chord cues it. Unreadable chords are outlined in red. Under each chord are the notes it will sound (or, with Follow my pitch off, the semitone shifts).

MIDI keyboard. Press Connect MIDI once; Chrome remembers it. Held notes become harmony voices, velocity sets their level and the sustain pedal holds them. Any device or channel works, alongside the laptop keys and the chart.

All three can sound at once, up to 8 voices in total. Keys you press light up amber on the on-screen piano; notes sounding from the chart or a MIDI keyboard glow a softer gold.

No pitch heard? (in the chord chart) is the note chords are voiced around whenever no pitch is being tracked — in silence, or with Follow my pitch off.

Input

Microphone. Pick your mic or audio interface under Device. On a two-input interface each socket is its own channel: set Channel to the input your mic is in, or leave it on Both inputs. Your choices are remembered.

Audio file. Switch Input to Audio file and choose or drop a file; p plays it, and everything else works on it just as on your voice. WAV, MP3, AAC/M4A, FLAC and OGG work. Apple Lossless .m4a does not — re-encode it as AAC. Pitch tracking follows one voice: on a full mix it stays steady but may lock onto the wrong note, so turn Follow my pitch off for those.

Your voice

The tuner shows the note you're singing and how far off it is: the needle turns green within 10 cents. The Pitch confidence bar at the bottom of the card shows how sure the tracker is.

Visualizer

Pitch scrolls through the last six seconds like a piano roll: the white line is the pitch you're singing, and each amber bar is a harmony voice at the note it's playing. The C lines are labelled so you can read octaves. It's the quickest way to see a chord land around your melody, or to spot yourself drifting off pitch. Wave is an oscilloscope of the output mix, held steady on each cycle and scaled up so quiet singing still shows.

Mix

Levels are volume multipliers: 1.00 is unchanged, 0.50 is half the signal, 0 is off, and Dry and Harmony can boost up to 1.50. Spread pans the harmony voices apart, from 0 (centred) to 1 (hard left and right). Detune nudges each voice a few cents sharp or flat for a thicker, doubled sound — 6–10¢ with a spread of 0.6–0.8 gives the wide Prismizer-style stack. Master, in the top bar, is only what you hear; recordings are taken before it. Double-click any slider, here or anywhere, to reset it.

Engine

Follow my pitch. On, a chord means real notes — Am is A, C and E wherever you sing. Off, the keys and chart are fixed intervals above your voice (middle C = your own pitch on a MIDI keyboard).

Preserve formants. On keeps the harmonies sounding like your voice (PSOLA). Off is the older resampling shifter — the chipmunk sound — kept for comparison. Very deep shifts (over an octave down) always use the resampler.

Fold octaves. On moves every harmony to the octave nearest your voice, so no voice is shifted more than half an octave — smoother, most natural. Off keeps the written voicing (root nearest you, the rest stacked above, slash bass below) and plays MIDI notes exactly as pressed. The octave is chosen when a chord or key starts, not continuously.

Lowest note. How low the pitch tracker listens. Reaching lower costs delay: C3 about 21 ms, E2 about 30 ms, C2 about 36 ms. The dry voice is delayed to match, so everything stays in sync.

The strip under the cards shows how many voices are sounding, the delay the pitch engine adds, your audio device's output latency and the sample rate.

Voice shaping

These shape the harmony voices only — your own voice is untouched. Everything starts neutral; Reset all puts it back, and double-clicking a slider resets just that one.

LFO is a slow, repeating wobble. Target picks what it moves: Pitch is vibrato, Volume is tremolo, Pan sweeps voices side to side, and Filter sweeps the filter's cutoff for a wah-like motion. Rate is how fast (cycles per second), Depth how far — at 1, pitch moves a full semitone either way. Shape is the wobble's curve; Random jumps to a new value each cycle. Every voice runs on its own phase, so a stack shimmers rather than wobbling in lockstep.

Envelope. Attack is how long a harmony takes to swell in, Release how long it rings after you let go — long attacks and releases give slow, pad-like swells. Glide makes voices slide to their new notes when the chord changes; it never slows how they follow your own pitch.

Filter is a low-pass on the harmonies: lower Cutoff is darker and softer, fully right is off. Resonance adds a peak at the cutoff, which sounds vocal and wah-like when swept by the LFO.

Formant shift moves the character of the harmony voices up (smaller, brighter, younger) or down (bigger, darker) without changing the notes — a signature Prismizer sound. It works with Preserve formants on.

Recording

Press Record or r to capture the full stereo mix; press again to save a 24-bit WAV to your Downloads folder. Pressing Stop mid-take saves it too. Recordings are unaffected by Master and mute, and are free of Bluetooth delay. About 17 MB per minute.

Troubleshooting

  • Harmonies lag behind. Bluetooth headphones add well over 100 ms; wired headphones fix most of it. Output latency in the strip under the cards shows what your device adds.
  • No sound from a mic or audio interface. Pick it under Device in the Input card. On a two-input interface (a UMC202HD, Scarlett 2i2 and so on), each socket is a separate channel: set Channel to the input your mic is in, or leave it on Both inputs. Then check the interface's gain knob, switch on +48V phantom power for a condenser mic, and make sure Chrome is allowed under System Settings › Privacy & Security › Microphone. The level bar in the top bar moves whenever sound is getting in.
  • Keys do nothing. Press Start first, and click outside the chord box so it isn't catching your typing.
  • Howling or squealing. That's feedback — press esc and switch to headphones.
  • MIDI keyboard not found. Check it appears in macOS's Audio MIDI Setup, then reload.
